## Rewritten Text

Phenotypic plasticity is plausible in PI3K-alpha inhibitor resistance, but the packet supports it most strongly in preclinical breast cancer models rather than in serial patient single-cell or lineage-tracing data. The clearest example is a chronic BYL719-resistant T47D system in which investigators explicitly compared BYL719-sensitive and BYL719-resistant cell states using quantitative modeling and phosphoproteomics. Resistant proliferating cells retained cycling despite BYL719 exposure, showed enrichment of cell-cycle checkpoint and DNA-damage response pathways, and selected for high p21. Functionally, high p21 favored repair of BYL719-induced DNA damage and bypass of drug-associated senescence; adding the CHK1 inhibitor MK-8776 preferentially killed p21-high resistant cells. This supports a stress-response and cell-cycle state model of resistance, but because it is centered on T47D-derived resistant cells, it should not be generalized as a universal lineage-adaptation program `pmid-38273040`.

A second, more explicitly plasticity-linked mechanism involves CD44 alternative splicing. In luminal, PIK3CA-mutant breast cancer models, adaptive resistance to BYL719 was associated with acquisition of a CD44-high state. The paper connected ER signaling, BYL719 exposure, CD44 isoform splicing, and downstream signaling: estradiol and BYL719 increased CD44 alternative splicing, fulvestrant suppressed it, CD44 knockdown attenuated BYL719-associated ER expression and ER target-gene induction, and enhanced CD44 interaction with hyaluronan activated Src-ERK signaling while maintaining AKT and mTOR activity in the presence of PI3K-alpha inhibition. Forced HAS2 expression reduced BYL719 sensitivity, whereas hyaluronidase or HAS inhibition partially restored drug response. This is stronger than generic plasticity language because it links a reversible cell-surface and ligand-interaction state to a drug-response phenotype `pmid-33024087`.

Other packet papers support adaptive signaling rewiring rather than lineage plasticity in the strict sense. HER2-overexpressing breast cancer models treated with PI3K-pathway inhibitors showed loss of AKT signaling but compensatory HER-family activation, HER3 induction, and ERK dependency; MEK inhibition or anti-HER2 therapy blocked ERK activation and improved anti-proliferative, pro-apoptotic, and in vivo effects `pmid-21278786`. A systematic BYL719 resistance screen found many validated resistance genes and, in a small paired biopsy set, measurable post-treatment upregulation of multiple resistance genes, implying heterogeneous transcriptional programs after therapy but requiring larger clinical cohorts for validation `pmid-27604488`. Rapid feedback changes in PTEN regulation also fit the adaptive-state theme: PI3K inhibition can inactivate PTEN through a USP10-GSK3beta mechanism `pmid-40991650`, and pharmacologic PI3K inhibition can reduce PTEN translation and permit AKT rebound in model systems `pmid-33606974`.

Overall, this subsection should be framed as an emerging, model-supported resistance class. The evidence supports drug-induced stress states, CD44-HA plasticity, and compensatory signaling feedback after PI3K-alpha blockade, but it does not yet establish a broad clinical lineage-adaptation program analogous to histologic transformation or stable fate switching.

## Evidence Use Notes

The rewrite narrows the draft from broad lineage adaptation to drug-induced adaptive states with direct PI3K-alpha inhibitor links. It separates stronger BYL719 p21 and CD44 evidence from broader feedback rewiring papers that support plasticity-like adaptation but not lineage switching.

## Residual Uncertainty

No packet paper provides definitive patient-derived single-cell evidence that a lineage state emerges during PI3K-alpha therapy and drives clinical progression. The most convincing support is preclinical, so clinical claims should remain cautious until serial biopsies, single-cell profiling, or functional patient-derived models connect these states to treatment timing and outcome.
