## Rewritten Text

HER2/ERBB and broader receptor tyrosine kinase crosstalk are among the best-supported bypass themes in this packet, but the evidence spans several different clinical directions. One direction asks whether PI3K-alpha or PI3K-pathway inhibition releases upstream ERBB signaling and thereby creates resistance to PI3K inhibition. In HER2-overexpressing breast cancer models, PI3K or PI3K/mTOR blockade inhibited AKT but increased HER2 and HER3 phosphorylation, HER3 transcription, HER2/HER3 dimerization, and HER2-GRB2 and HER3-p85 adaptor binding. The same perturbation produced ERK activation; lapatinib, trastuzumab, or MEK inhibitors suppressed this ERK rebound and improved proliferation, apoptosis, and xenograft growth endpoints compared with PI3K-pathway inhibition alone `pmid-21278786`. A genetic in vivo model reinforced that this feedback can matter after an initial response: Her2 plus PIK3CA H1047R mammary tumors regressed after mutant PIK3CA deinduction, but recurrent tumors restored Her2/Her3 and MAPK signaling, and lapatinib plus MEK162 produced sustained regression in recurrent transplants `pmid-26640141`.

A second direction asks whether PI3K activation itself explains resistance to HER2 therapy and therefore motivates HER2 plus PI3K cotargeting. In HER2-positive PTEN-deficient breast tumor models, p110-alpha was described as a major effector downstream of RTKs, and combined Her2, p110-alpha, and p110-beta inhibition most strongly reduced AKT activation and minimized tumor growth `pmid-26500061`. Other HER2-positive preclinical studies found that adding PI3K inhibitors, including alpelisib or GDC-0077, could circumvent trastuzumab resistance in PIK3CA-mutant or PTEN-deficient cell models, with increased apoptosis and in vivo synergy reported, although the clinical translatability and toxicity of such combinations remain unsettled `pmid-35968355`. A phase 1 study moved the concept into patients with PIK3CA-mutated HER2-positive metastatic breast cancer by combining alpelisib, trastuzumab, and the HER3 antibody LJM716. This trial supports feasibility of HER2-HER3-PI3K cotargeting, but it was primarily a safety and dose-finding study and emphasizes overlapping toxicity rather than definitive resistance reversal `pmid-33947692`.

The packet also shows that RTK crosstalk should not be narrowed to HER2 alone. In luminal and triple-negative breast cancer models, NRG1, EGF, and FGF10 protected cells from BYL719 and reactivated AKT, S6, 4E-BP1, and ERK; SHP2 overexpression conferred resistance, while SHP2 inhibition, knockout, or phosphatase-dead reconstitution experiments showed that SHP2 was needed for RTK-mediated resistance and that PI3K plus SHP2 inhibition suppressed organoid and xenograft growth `pmid-35365185`. In squamous carcinoma models, acquired BYL719 resistance involved AXL overexpression, AXL-EGFR interaction, and downstream PLC-gamma-PKC signaling that maintained mTOR despite PI3K-AKT inhibition; resistance was reversed by combining PI3K-alpha inhibition with EGFR, AXL, or PKC blockade `pmid-25873175`. EGFR ligand signaling can also drive acquired PI3K inhibitor resistance outside breast cancer, as SW48 colorectal models selected in GDC-0941 secreted amphiregulin, maintained EGFR-MAPK signaling, and gained sensitivity to EGFR or MAPK cotargeting `pmid-25053989`. Likewise, IGF1R upregulation maintained AKT-mTOR signaling and conferred acquired resistance to GDC0032 or BYL719 in PIK3CA-driven ovarian cancer, with IGF1R inhibition restoring sensitivity `pmid-30237504`. Thus, the review should present HER2/ERBB crosstalk as a particularly developed example of a broader RTK feedback problem, supported mostly by preclinical mechanism and early combination trials rather than by definitive biomarker-selected clinical resistance studies.

## Evidence Use Notes

The draft warned against drifting into general HER2 biology. The packet supports a more concrete rewrite by separating three evidence streams: HER2 feedback after PI3K inhibition, PI3K dependence as a cause of anti-HER2 resistance, and broader RTK bypass circuits involving SHP2, AXL-EGFR, EGFR ligands, and IGF1R. The strongest clinical evidence is feasibility of HER2-HER3-PI3K cotargeting, not definitive proof of clinical reversal.

## Residual Uncertainty

The packet includes limited mature patient biomarker evidence linking ERBB or RTK activation to outcomes on approved PI3K-alpha regimens. Several useful studies are outside breast cancer or use pan-PI3K, dual PI3K/mTOR, or investigational agents. Later verification should avoid claiming that all ERBB-active tumors require PI3K-alpha plus HER2 therapy unless supported by setting-specific clinical data.
