## Rewritten Text

PIK3CA alteration is best treated as the therapeutic entry point for PI3Kalpha inhibition, not as an all-purpose explanation for resistance. The strongest clinical anchor is SOLAR-1: men and postmenopausal women with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer whose disease had recurred or progressed on or after aromatase inhibitor therapy were randomized to alpelisib plus fulvestrant or placebo plus fulvestrant, and the PIK3CA-mutated cohort had longer progression-free survival with the alpelisib regimen. That result supports PIK3CA mutation as a selection biomarker for approved alpelisib use, but it does not by itself specify why an individual tumor later escapes treatment `pmid-31091374`.

The target rationale is biologically plausible because oncogenic PIK3CA alterations activate PI3K pathway signaling and can create dependence on p110alpha. In the preclinical BYL719 characterization, PIK3CA-mutant cancer models showed selective sensitivity to PI3Kalpha inhibition, pharmacodynamic suppression of pathway signaling, and antitumor activity in PIK3CA-dependent xenografts. This establishes why a mutant pathway node was attractive for drug development and patient stratification, while leaving open which co-alterations or adaptive states determine incomplete response `pmid-24608574`. Regulatory evidence then narrows the clinical claim: the FDA approval summary defines the indication as HR-positive, HER2-negative, PIK3CA-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer detected by an FDA-approved test after progression on or after endocrine-based therapy `pmid-33168657`.

The entry-point claim also needs important boundaries. A later SOLAR-1 genomic analysis found that alpelisib plus fulvestrant did not show comparable efficacy in PIK3CA-wild-type disease and reinforced PIK3CA alteration as the main predictive biomarker in that trial context. The same analysis showed heterogeneity among PIK3CA-altered tumors, including differences by tumor mutational burden, PTEN co-mutation, PIK3CA amplification, and multiple PIK3CA mutations, so PIK3CA positivity should not be read as uniform pathway dependence `pmid-41967638`. In SANDPIPER, ctDNA-based analysis of taselisib plus fulvestrant suggested that multiple PIK3CA mutation clonality further stratified outcomes, with clonal multiple mutations associated with greater PI3K inhibitor sensitivity than subclonal patterns `pmid-37101291`. Noncanonical PIK3CA alterations add similar nuance: C-terminal frameshift mutations activated AKT and S6 signaling in engineered models and were sensitive to PI3Kalpha inhibition, but such model-system evidence does not automatically define clinical resistance behavior `pmid-32929011`.

Finally, the packet cautions against assuming that PIK3CA mutation predicts benefit equally across tumor types or treatment histories. In NCI-MATCH subprotocol I, taselisib in PIK3CA-mutated advanced solid tumors outside breast and squamous lung cancer produced limited objective activity despite selecting for activating PIK3CA mutations, suggesting that histology, co-alterations, and treatment context matter `pmid-35138919`. A six-patient recurrent or advanced cervical cancer alpelisib series suggested possible disease control in PIK3CA-mutated cases, but the uncontrolled size makes it hypothesis-generating rather than definitive `pmid-35311394`. Therefore, this subsection should frame PIK3CA alteration as the reason PI3Kalpha inhibition is clinically and biologically testable, while reserving resistance claims for evidence that links treatment exposure to progression, acquired alterations, pathway reactivation, or functional escape.

## Evidence Use Notes

The packet converted the draft from a generic target-rationale statement into a narrower distinction between selection biomarker evidence, preclinical pathway-dependence evidence, and resistance evidence. SOLAR-1, the FDA approval summary, and the BYL719 characterization support the entry point. Genomic SOLAR-1, SANDPIPER clonality, C-terminal frameshift, NCI-MATCH, and cervical cancer papers add heterogeneity and boundary conditions.

## Residual Uncertainty

The work order included a 2026 testing-consensus citation in the draft, but that paper was not in the selected Stage 7 packet and therefore was not cited. Prevalence outside HR-positive HER2-negative breast cancer is only indirectly represented here, and later stages should verify large tumor-agnostic prevalence sources if the final review needs cancer-type frequency claims.
