## Rewritten Text

Biomarker strategy for PI3K-alpha inhibition should be organized around the decision being made. Baseline PIK3CA mutation is a treatment-selection biomarker in HR-positive HER2-negative advanced breast cancer because SOLAR-1 tested alpelisib plus fulvestrant in a biomarker-defined population and showed improved progression-free survival compared with fulvestrant alone in the PIK3CA-mutant cohort. That does not mean that every PIK3CA alteration has identical predictive value, that PIK3CA mutation is sufficient to predict durable benefit, or that baseline mutation explains later resistance. The more recent SOLAR-1 genomic determinant analysis and the original BYL719 development work are therefore best treated as trial-level and development-level evidence for refining baseline selection, assay interpretation, and co-alteration questions, rather than as proof that a single binary PIK3CA test captures the full response biology; smaller combination studies such as alpelisib plus nab-paclitaxel also show that biomarker interpretation depends on the treatment backbone being tested `pmid-31091374` `pmid-41967638` `pmid-24608574` `pmid-33602685`.

Acquired resistance biomarkers require a different evidence standard: paired or serial samples, a known drug exposure, and an endpoint tied to progression. Varkaris and colleagues used serial ctDNA and rapid-autopsy profiling from patients with PIK3CA-mutated HR-positive HER2-negative advanced breast cancer treated with alpelisib or inavolisib and found acquired PI3K-pathway alterations in half of the serially profiled cases, including secondary PIK3CA mutations, PTEN loss-of-function alterations, and activating AKT1 alterations. This supports post-progression testing as more than a cataloging exercise: secondary catalytic-pocket PIK3CA mutations may indicate resistance to orthosteric PI3K-alpha inhibitors but retained sensitivity to allosteric RLY-2608 in models, whereas AKT1 alterations created a different therapeutic question because they were associated with cross-resistance to orthosteric and allosteric PI3K-alpha inhibition in the same study `pmid-37916958`.

Other packet papers make the biomarker landscape broader and less cleanly clinical. Juric-associated longitudinal trial profiling of alpelisib plus aromatase inhibitors found PTEN loss-of-function mutations in resistant cases and expansion of ESR1 activating mutations, linking PI3K-alpha resistance with endocrine pressure and spatially heterogeneous tumor evolution `pmid-32864625`. Functional studies nominate additional candidate biomarkers: PIM kinase upregulation maintained downstream PI3K effector activity in BYL719-resistant breast cancer cells and was seen in a small paired-biopsy set, but the authors explicitly framed the clinical biopsy evidence as preliminary `pmid-27604488`; persistent FOXM1 expression after PI3K-alpha inhibition marked resistance in ER-positive breast cancer models and drove LDH-dependent metabolic changes detectable by hyperpolarized carbon-13 pyruvate-lactate MRI rather than FDG-PET, making it a pharmacodynamic and imaging biomarker candidate `pmid-32976773`; integrative modeling of BYL719-sensitive and resistant T47D states nominated PDK1 co-targeting and a p21-high resistance phenotype, but this is a prioritization framework rather than a validated clinical test `pmid-38273040`. RTK-expression studies, IGF1R/p110-beta rewiring, NF1-loss models, and mTORC1-activating co-alterations support exploratory biomarkers for combination choice, but most remain model-based or translational rather than clinically validated `pmid-28382169` `pmid-27048245` `pmid-37044095` `pmid-33685991`.

The practical review claim should therefore be disciplined: biomarker language should specify whether the marker is predictive, acquired-resistance-associated, prognostic, pharmacodynamic, or exploratory. Taselisib studies illustrate why this matters. In SANDPIPER and related translational analyses, PIK3CA mutation status and ctDNA-defined features were associated with differential benefit from taselisib plus fulvestrant, yet the regimen’s modest benefit and toxicity limited clinical utility, so a biomarker-positive subgroup did not rescue the drug as a treatment strategy `pmid-36892268`. Complex PIK3CA genotypes, including multiple mutations or rare C-terminal frameshift alterations, may eventually refine PI3K-alpha sensitivity claims, but this packet supports treating them as selection-context evidence until tied prospectively to outcomes under specific inhibitors `pmid-32494066` `pmid-32929011`.

## Evidence Use Notes

The rewrite converts the draft’s broad biomarker warning into operational categories: baseline treatment selection, acquired resistance, pharmacodynamic/imaging markers, and exploratory combination-selection markers. The packet supports baseline PIK3CA testing most strongly in SOLAR-1, acquired resistance profiling most strongly in serial ctDNA and longitudinal trial analyses, and exploratory biomarker hypotheses through model-system studies.

## Residual Uncertainty

The packet includes recent 2026 biomarker papers and several PDF-normalized sources whose subgroup cutoffs and tables should be rechecked in final verification. The De Angelis 2026 testing consensus cited in the original draft was not present as a selected packet paper here, so this rewrite does not cite it. Clinical utility for FOXM1 imaging, RTK expression, NF1 loss, PIM upregulation, and rare PIK3CA variants remains unresolved without prospective validation.
