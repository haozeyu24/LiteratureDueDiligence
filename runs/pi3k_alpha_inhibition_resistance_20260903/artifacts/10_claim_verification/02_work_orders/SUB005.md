### PTEN Loss And p110-beta Compensation

<!-- source_subsection_id: SUB005 -->

PTEN loss is one of the clearest PI3K-alpha inhibitor resistance mechanisms, but the evidence supports a stratified claim rather than a universal rule. The most direct example links therapy exposure, spatial tumor evolution, and functional rescue. In a patient with PIK3CA-mutant metastatic breast cancer treated with BYL719, a confirmed partial response lasted 9.5 months before lung progression. Rapid autopsy sequencing of 14 metastatic sites found single-copy PTEN loss across metastases and additional heterogeneous PTEN alterations in many progressing lesions; immunohistochemistry connected bi-allelic PTEN alteration with loss of PTEN protein in progressing lung and liver lesions, while a stable peri-aortic lesion retained PTEN protein. The same paper reported acquired bi-allelic PTEN loss in an additional BYL719-treated patient. Functionally, inducible PTEN knockdown in BYL719-sensitive T47D and MCF7 models restored AKT, PRAS40, GSK3-beta, and S6 signaling despite p110-alpha inhibition, and resistance was reversed only by combined p110-alpha and p110-beta blockade. A PTEN-null patient-derived xenograft was resistant to BYL719 but responded to pan-PI3K inhibition or the BYL719 plus AZD6482 p110-beta combination `pmid-25409150`.

Clinical cohort data broaden this beyond a single autopsy case while also showing heterogeneity. In a phase I/II study of alpelisib plus aromatase inhibition in HR-positive metastatic breast cancer, paired tissue and ctDNA profiling found PTEN alterations in 8 of 32 evaluable resistant patients. Three had pre-treatment loss-of-function PTEN alterations and rapid progression, while five had post-treatment PTEN loss-of-function mutations or homozygous deletion absent from pre-treatment samples. PTEN and ESR1 alterations together marked nearly half of profiled progressing cases, showing that resistance can arise through the PI3K pathway, the endocrine-therapy component, or both `pmid-32864625`. A later cohort of patients progressing on alpelisib or inavolisib similarly found acquired PI3K pathway alterations in about half of serial ctDNA cases, including PTEN loss-of-function alterations in 5 of 32 and PTEN alteration in 1 of 8 rapid-autopsy series, but also AKT1, secondary PIK3CA, FGFR, KRAS, and BRAF changes `pmid-37916958`.

p110-beta compensation should therefore be described as an important PTEN-linked and adaptive route, not simply as the only downstream escape. In BYL719-resistant PIK3CA-mutant breast cancer cell lines, resistance emerged without a difference in PTEN expression; phosphotyrosine proteomics and co-immunoprecipitation instead identified an IGF1R/IRS/p85/p110-beta complex that maintained AKT/mTOR/S6K signaling under p110-alpha blockade. IGF1R inhibition disrupted the complex, and either IGF1R or p110-beta inhibition restored BYL719 sensitivity in vitro and improved tumor control in MCF7 xenografts `pmid-27048245`. Non-genetic PTEN inactivation adds another layer: PI3K inhibitor exposure can activate a USP10-GSK3-beta axis that phosphorylates PTEN, blocks dimerization, maintains PIP3 and downstream signaling, and can be countered by USP10 or GSK3-beta suppression in resistant models and patient-derived organoids `pmid-40991650`. Because PTEN status has been defined by copy number, mutation, protein IHC, and downstream phosphoprotein readouts across studies, later verification should keep baseline PTEN loss, acquired genetic PTEN loss, adaptive p110-beta signaling, and post-translational PTEN inactivation as separate evidence classes `pmid-31859350`. Systematic resistance work reinforces this caution: PI3K inhibitor escape in breast cancer is heterogeneous, and post-resistance biopsies may show multiple resistance programs rather than one dominant lesion `pmid-27604488`.
