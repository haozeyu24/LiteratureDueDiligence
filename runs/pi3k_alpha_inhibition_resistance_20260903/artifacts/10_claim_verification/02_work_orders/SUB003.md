```markdown
### Inavolisib And The Clinical Triplet Context

<!-- source_subsection_id: SUB003 -->

Inavolisib should be presented as a clinically advanced PI3K-alpha inhibitor tested in a defined triplet regimen, not as generic proof that newer PI3K-alpha inhibitors overcome alpelisib resistance. In the phase III INAVO120 setting, patients had PIK3CA-mutated HR-positive HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer with recurrence or progression during or within 12 months after completing adjuvant endocrine therapy, and they had not simply been selected as a post-alpelisib population. The final overall-survival report randomized 161 patients to inavolisib plus palbociclib and fulvestrant and 164 to placebo plus palbociclib and fulvestrant. After median follow-up of about 34 and 32 months, median OS was 34.0 versus 27.0 months, the updated PFS hazard ratio was 0.42, and objective response was 62.7 percent versus 28.0 percent. These data support efficacy for the first-line endocrine-resistant triplet context, while leaving open whether the regimen treats tumors already resistant to prior PI3K-alpha inhibitors `pmid-40454641`.

The development path helps explain why the triplet could be delivered. In the phase I/Ib GO39374 study, inavolisib with palbociclib and endocrine therapy was reported to have manageable safety, no major drug-drug interactions, and preliminary durable antitumor activity in PIK3CA-mutated HR-positive HER2-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer. That study explicitly supported moving the inavolisib, palbociclib, and fulvestrant combination into INAVO120 rather than proving cross-resistance reversal `pmid-39236276`. The dedicated INAVO120 safety analysis further described inavolisib 9 mg daily with full-dose palbociclib and fulvestrant, tracking selected PI3K-inhibitor adverse events such as hyperglycemia, stomatitis or mucosal inflammation, rash, and diarrhea. These toxicities were more common with inavolisib than placebo but were generally characterized as manageable with supportive care and dose modification, with low treatment-discontinuation rates and no new unexpected safety signals `pmid-42202490`.

Mechanistically, the packet supports a more precise claim than simple "more selective inhibitor." Preclinical work on GDC-0077, now inavolisib, found that certain PI3K-alpha inhibitors can promote degradation of mutant p110-alpha, with stronger and more durable PI3K pathway suppression than nondegrading inhibitors in models, and combination experiments supported concurrent targeting of PI3K, ER, and CDK4/6 pathways `pmid-34544753`. A related commentary framed this as RTK-dependent mutant PI3K-alpha degradation that may reduce feedback-mediated reactivation and widen the therapeutic index, but this remains mechanistic rationale rather than a direct clinical resistance-overcoming claim `pmid-35022207`. Later first-in-human and translational analyses add nuance: single-agent inavolisib showed pharmacodynamic modulation and antitumor activity in PIK3CA-mutated solid tumors, and FGFR2 hotspot mutations in ctDNA were associated with clinical benefit, while FGFR2-selective inhibitor combinations delayed resistance in preclinical models `pmid-41143603`.

The strongest boundary comes from acquired-resistance evidence. A serial ctDNA and rapid-autopsy study of patients with advanced HR-positive HER2-negative breast cancer resistant to orthosteric PI3K-alpha inhibitors included both alpelisib- and inavolisib-treated patients and found acquired PI3K-pathway alterations in about half of patients, including secondary PIK3CA mutations affecting the inhibitor binding pocket. The study's proposed way around these on-target secondary mutations was an allosteric PI3K-alpha inhibitor, not inavolisib itself `pmid-37916958`. Therefore, INAVO120 should be used to support inavolisib triplet efficacy, safety, and combination feasibility in its studied first-line endocrine-resistant population. Claims that inavolisib overcomes alpelisib resistance, or overcomes defined secondary PIK3CA resistance mutations, should be rejected unless later evidence specifically enrolls such patients and measures those mechanisms.
```
