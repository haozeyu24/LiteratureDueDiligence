## Rewritten Text

Secondary PIK3CA mutations should be treated as a specific, on-target resistance class, not as a synonym for all multiple PIK3CA genotypes. The strongest packet evidence comes from patients with PIK3CA-mutant advanced HR-positive HER2-negative breast cancer who developed resistance to orthosteric PI3K-alpha inhibitors. In serial ctDNA from 32 patients treated with inavolisib or alpelisib, acquired PI3K pathway alterations appeared in 50 percent of cases, including secondary PIK3CA mutations beyond the pretreatment mutation in 9 of 32, PTEN loss-of-function alterations in 5 of 32, and activating AKT1 alterations in 5 of 32. Rapid-autopsy whole-exome sequencing of 100 lesions from 8 patients likewise found acquired PIK3CA alterations in 3 of 8 cases. In one inavolisib-treated patient, phylogenetic analysis showed truncal H1047R and E726K with subclonal Q859K and AKT1 alterations at autopsy; in serial ddPCR from two patients, emergence of Q859K, W780R, and AKT1 mutations accompanied rebound of the founder H1047R mutation before or around clinical progression `pmid-37916958`.

Mechanistically, the acquired W780 and Q859 substitutions are plausible resistance mutations because they affect the catalytic-pocket interactions used by orthosteric inhibitors. Structural modeling and free-energy perturbation predicted that Q859H or Q859K would disrupt the hydrogen-bonding interaction important for PI3K-alpha-selective orthosteric inhibitors such as alpelisib and inavolisib, whereas W780R would affect a residue conserved across PI3K isoforms and impair both alpha-selective and pan-PI3K ATP-competitive inhibitors. Engineered T47D or MCF7 cells expressing primary activating PIK3CA mutations plus W780R or Q859H/K showed increased IC50 values for alpelisib and inavolisib, with differential effects on pictilisib and taselisib. RLY-2608, an allosteric pan-mutant-selective inhibitor, retained potency, binding, and downstream signaling inhibition in these double-mutant models because it binds outside the catalytic pocket `pmid-37916958`. The RLY-2608 discovery paper supports the plausibility of this binding-mode distinction, but its clinical evidence consists of early proof-of-concept responses in patients with kinase- or helical-domain PIK3CA-mutant breast cancer, not a demonstrated clinical rescue cohort after secondary PIK3CA resistance `pmid-37916956`.

This evidence must be separated from baseline multiple PIK3CA mutations. In SANDPIPER ctDNA analyses of HR-positive HER2-negative metastatic breast cancer, clonal multiple PIK3CA mutations correlated with higher response and longer progression-free survival on taselisib plus fulvestrant than subclonal multiple mutations, supporting the idea that some baseline double mutations mark stronger pathway dependence rather than resistance `pmid-37101291`. Mutation-spectrum studies similarly show that double PIK3CA mutations are not rare among PIK3CA-mutant breast cancers and that non-hotspot mutations such as E726K may act differently depending on whether they are isolated, paired with hotspot mutations, clonal, or subclonal `pmid-32404150`; comprehensive genomic profiling extends this point by showing that OTHERm and SOLAR1m mutations overlap clinically, that some subclonal OTHERm may represent later tumor evolution, and that clinical benefit from alpelisib can still occur outside the original hotspot set `pmid-36321996`. Thus, the practical review claim is narrow: acquired W780 and Q859 mutations provide direct on-target resistance evidence for orthosteric PI3K-alpha inhibitors, while baseline double or non-hotspot PIK3CA mutations require separate interpretation. Because PI3K inhibitor resistance also includes PTEN loss, AKT1 activation, RTK/MAPK bypass, p110-beta compensation, and adaptive transcriptional programs, serial ctDNA or contemporary tissue profiling is essential before assigning progression to secondary PIK3CA mutation alone `pmid-31859350` `pmid-27604488`.

## Evidence Use Notes

The packet converted this subsection from a speculative prompt into a narrow mechanistic synthesis. The direct secondary PIK3CA resistance claim rests mainly on `pmid-37916958`. Other papers were used to prevent conflating acquired on-treatment W780/Q859 mutations with baseline double PIK3CA mutations, non-hotspot variants, or broader biomarker heterogeneity.

## Residual Uncertainty

The key uncertainty is clinical actionability after progression: the packet does not show patients with acquired W780 or Q859 mutations subsequently responding to RLY-2608 or another allosteric inhibitor. Allele phase, subclonality, co-occurring AKT1 or PTEN alterations, and assay timing remain important claim-verification issues.
