## Rewritten Text

STX-478 and RLY-2608 sharpen the PI3K-alpha inhibitor question from simple pathway blockade to pharmacologic selectivity: can a drug inhibit mutant p110-alpha deeply enough for antitumor activity while sparing wild-type PI3K-alpha functions that drive hyperglycemia, compensatory insulin release, rash, and combination intolerance? The structural rationale predates these agents. Fragment-crystallography work on PI3K-alpha emphasized that the wild-type and mutant ATP-binding sites are highly similar, making ATP-competitive mutant selectivity difficult, and identified non-ATP allosteric pockets, including regions near helical-domain hotspot residues, as plausible starting points for mutant-specific design `pmid-28129991`. A later commentary framed the same therapeutic-index problem biologically: systemic wild-type PI3K-alpha inhibition impairs glucose control and can provoke insulin feedback that reactivates tumor PI3K signaling, so sparing wild-type enzyme is a mechanistically coherent but still testable solution `pmid-35022207`.

The primary STX-478 evidence remains preclinical. In its discovery paper, STX-478 is presented as an allosteric, mutant-selective PI3K-alpha inhibitor binding a cryptic site distinct from the ATP pocket. The study reports faster binding kinetics for mutant enzymes, selective cellular activity across kinase-domain and helical-domain PIK3CA-mutant lines, and xenograft activity in breast, head and neck, colon, and lung models. Importantly for therapeutic index, STX-478 produced antitumor effects comparable or superior to high-dose alpelisib in mutant xenografts while preserving glucose uptake in human adipocytes and avoiding in vivo insulin resistance and metabolic dysregulation seen with alpelisib. Combination studies with fulvestrant and/or CDK4/6 inhibitors produced durable regressions in ER-positive HER2-negative breast cancer xenografts without overt mouse tolerability signals, but these are not yet proof that clinical resistance has been solved `pmid-37623743`.

RLY-2608 has both discovery and early clinical proof-of-concept data. The RLY-2608 paper used molecular dynamics, cryo-EM, and DNA-encoded-library screening to identify a mutant-selective allosteric inhibitor. In PIK3CA-mutant xenografts, RLY-2608 inhibited tumor growth with minimal insulin increase, and two patients with advanced HR-positive breast cancer harboring kinase- or helical-domain PIK3CA mutations had objective responses without observed wild-type PI3K-alpha toxicities in the reported vignettes `pmid-37916956`. The more resistance-specific evidence comes from a separate acquired-resistance study: patients progressing after alpelisib or inavolisib developed secondary PIK3CA alterations, including W780 and Q859 catalytic-pocket mutations, and engineered breast cancer models carrying these double mutants showed reduced sensitivity to orthosteric PI3K-alpha inhibitors. RLY-2608 retained potency and signaling inhibition in those models because it binds outside the catalytic pocket `pmid-37916958`. Thus, the defensible claim is not that mutant-selective inhibitors have clinically overcome resistance, but that STX-478 and RLY-2608 provide a coherent design strategy with preclinical therapeutic-index evidence, early RLY-2608 clinical activity, and model-based evidence that allosteric binding can bypass some orthosteric on-target resistance mutations.

## Evidence Use Notes

The packet changed the subsection from a search-plan placeholder into a more specific evidence synthesis. STX-478 is supported mainly by preclinical selectivity, pharmacodynamic, metabolic, xenograft, and combination data. RLY-2608 has preclinical and two-patient proof-of-concept evidence, plus separate model-based evidence against clinically observed secondary PIK3CA mutations. The rewrite narrows all claims so clinical resistance resolution is not implied.
