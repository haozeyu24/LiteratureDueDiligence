# Structured Review Instruction

## Objective

- objective: Produce a claim-verifiable biomedical review of resistance mechanisms to PI3K-alpha inhibition in cancer treatment, integrating laboratory mechanisms, patient-derived evidence, and clinical trial outcomes.
- why this review is needed: The user wants to understand the many potential resistance mechanisms across approved and investigational PI3K-alpha inhibitors, including how successful and failed clinical trials may inform combination strategies intended to overcome PI3K inhibitor resistance.

## Downstream Use And Audience

- downstream use: inferred manuscript planning, scientific review drafting, and translational oncology due diligence.
- likely audience: cancer biologists, translational oncology researchers, clinical oncologists, and drug-development readers.
- expertise level: expert or semi-expert biomedical audience familiar with targeted therapy, PIK3CA biology, pathway signaling, biomarkers, and clinical trial terminology.

## Desired Review Product

- product type: structured review draft followed by evidence retrieval, claim verification, and final reader-facing synthesis.
- expected depth: broad and evidence-centered, covering mechanistic studies, patient-derived resistance evidence, clinical trials, inhibitor-specific distinctions, negative or failed studies, and rational combinations.
- target length or paper count: unspecified; use broad relevant coverage rather than a fixed paper-count target.
- required tables, figures, or special sections: inferred useful outputs include an inhibitor landscape table, clinical trial evidence table, resistance-mechanism matrix, and combination-strategy summary; these are useful review features rather than mandatory artifacts unless later requested.

## Claim Verification Rules

### Evidence That Supports A Claim

- direct evidence: treated patient samples, clinical trial biomarker analyses, serial ctDNA/biopsy/autopsy, resistance-selected models, perturbation/rescue experiments, or cross-drug assays showing a mechanism after PI3K-alpha inhibitor exposure.
- indirect evidence: pathway or biomarker findings plausibly connected to PI3K-alpha inhibitor resistance but lacking direct treatment-timing or rescue evidence; use cautious language.
- review-level evidence: acceptable for background, landscape, and citation discovery; insufficient alone for primary resistance claims.
- acceptable use of negative or failed results: negative or failed trials can support statements about efficacy limits, tolerability, feasibility, trial design, or unmet need; do not treat them as proof of biological resistance unless mechanistic data support that link.

### Evidence That Is Insufficient By Itself

- co-occurrence-only signals: alteration and cancer co-occurrence without treatment timing.
- background-only signals: pathway activation or PIK3CA prevalence without PI3K-alpha inhibitor exposure.
- association-only signals: baseline biomarkers without outcome, temporal, or functional evidence.
- review-only signals: review claims without primary-source support.

### Full-Text Handling

- full-text review requirement: prefer full text for mechanistic, trial-design, biomarker, comparative, and causal claims.
- title/abstract fallback: title/abstract-only evidence may be used with explicit access labels when full text is unavailable.
- missing full-text queue: create a user-download queue for papers where full text is needed but unavailable.
- claims that require full text before final acceptance: acquired resistance mechanisms, patient-derived resistance claims, trial failure explanations, biomarker subgroup interpretation, and claims that combinations overcome resistance.

### Claims Requiring Extra Scrutiny

- broad mechanism claims: avoid universal wording across inhibitors, tumor types, or treatment settings without direct support.
- causal claims: require timing, perturbation, rescue, serial sampling, or strong translational evidence.
- biomedical/translational claims: distinguish model-system results from patient evidence and trial outcomes.
- comparative or superiority claims: require appropriate comparative evidence.
- citation-sensitive claims: trial phase, regimen, eligibility, endpoints, toxicity, biomarker subgroup, resistance mechanism, and failure reason.

### Citation Risk Areas

- likely citation traps: conflating pan-PI3K data with PI3K-alpha-selective data; conflating endocrine resistance with PI3K-alpha inhibitor resistance; treating toxicity-driven discontinuation as tumor escape; overstating preclinical evidence for investigational inhibitors.
- papers that may be easy to confuse: alpelisib/BYL719 reports across settings; inavolisib/GDC-0077 reports; STX478/STX-478; RLy2608/RLY-2608; SOLAR-1, BYLieve, INAVO120, and other trial reports.
- claims likely to need full text: secondary PIK3CA mutations, PTEN loss, pathway reactivation, ctDNA emergence, trial biomarker analysis, and combination rationale.

## Review Structure Guidance

- desired chapter order: choose a scope-driven structure that covers clinical rationale, inhibitor landscape, laboratory and patient-derived resistance mechanisms, clinical trial evidence, combination strategies, and future questions.
- required recurring distinctions: intrinsic versus acquired resistance; laboratory versus patient evidence; preclinical versus clinical evidence; approved versus investigational inhibitors; resistance biology versus toxicity or trial design; monotherapy versus combination therapy.

## Uncertainty And Controversy Guidance

- unresolved questions: prevalence of each mechanism; clinical durability of next-generation inhibitors; whether mutant-selective inhibitors overcome prior PI3K-alpha inhibitor resistance in patients; optimal combinations and sequencing.
- competing explanations: genetic escape, adaptive signaling, endocrine resistance, clonal heterogeneity, toxicity-limited exposure, incomplete pathway suppression, and trial design.
- known limitations: broad topic, rapidly evolving investigational literature, incomplete full-text access, and preclinical-to-clinical translation uncertainty.
